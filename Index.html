Mosque Governance & Statutory Learning Cards

الوحدات التعليمية والإدارية
Section 28 • Waqf Act 1941 First Priority

Asset Preservation & "First Charge" Rule

Under Section 28 of the Waqf Act, structural maintenance and property repairs are the first financial charge on any income generated by a mosque or attached Waqf storefronts. Managers cannot redirect building capital into secondary outreach if physical repairs are needed.

Routine: Infrastructure Care
Sections 38A, 39 & 41 Strict Prohibition

Asset Alienation & Collateral Restrictions

Mosque administrators act solely as legal custodians (Amanah). Selling, mortgaging waqf land as bank collateral, or executing long-term leases (>3 yrs for houses, >9 yrs for land) without prior Board or Supreme Court sanction is strictly illegal and void.

Routine: Commercial Leases
Section 44 • April 1st Annual Audit

Mandatory Financial Audits & Filing

Every Mutawalli must maintain audited ledger records for Zakat, Sadaqah, and cash box collections (Tabung), officially submitting certified accounts to the Board of Waqf Commissioners by the April 1st statutory deadline.

Routine: Ledger Compilation
AML/CFT & FCC Protocols Compliance

AML / CFT Compliance & Donor Verification

Modern statutory mandates require mosques to log beneficial ownership, perform due diligence on large anonymous transfers or overseas donors, and report suspicious transactions to national financial crime regulators.

Routine: Cash Box & Foreign Transfers
Sections 36 & 37 • Waqf Act 10% Ceiling

Mutawalli Remuneration Ceiling (10% Cap)

To protect public funds, the law caps administrative remuneration for mosque managers at a maximum of one-tenth (10%) of the gross waqf income. Exceeding this allowance requires explicit judicial authorization from the Supreme Court.

Routine: Committee Stipends
Statutory Penalties Enforcement

Statutory Penalties & Committee Removal

Governance failures trigger immediate legal consequences: statutory fines for missing filings, forced removal of committee members by the Board under Section 34, and up to 6 months imprisonment for criminal breach of trust.

Enforcement: Waqf & FCC Act
Statutory Bodies

1. Mauritian Legal Governance Framework

الإطار القانوني والتنظيمي

The state plays an active role in regulating religious institutions to ensure financial transparency, protect communal assets, and prevent inter-communal friction through statutory bodies established by Parliament:

The Waqf Board (Waqf Act 1941)

Oversees all Muslim waqfs (charitable endowments, real estate properties, and mosques). While mosques are managed locally by individual Mutawallis (trustees), all balance sheets must be turned over to this statutory board.

Statutory Body 1941

Muslim Family Council (MFC)

Formally registers Islamic marriages (Nikah) and manages essential elements of Islamic family law. This enables the secular state to track civil statuses while preserving minority religious customs.

Family Law & Civil Status

Islamic Cultural Centre (ICC)

Created via an Act of Parliament to coordinate communal heritage, manage logistics for the annual Hajj pilgrimage, and represent Muslim cultural affairs within the broader multi-faith state.

Parliamentary Act

2. Confronting Factors & Tension Points

التحديات والتوازنات الداخلية
1

AML/CFT Compliance vs. Autonomy

Recent amendments to the Waqf Act expanded state authority to enforce Anti-Money Laundering (AML) and Counter-Terrorism Financing (CFT) compliance. While aimed at international credibility, local Mutawallis cite excessive administrative burdens on local charities.

2

Sectarian & Ideological Diversity

The community exhibits historical divisions primarily between Sunnat Jama'at (Barelwi) and Tablighi Jama'at / Jamiat-Ul-Ulama (Deobandi). Disputes arise over mosque property control, requiring the Waqf Board to maintain strict neutrality.

3

Religious Subsidies vs. Neutrality

The Mauritian government allocates state financial subsidies to registered religious groups proportionally based on census data. This requires the secular state to monitor religious affiliation, sparking continuous debates on equity.

Annual Mosque Regulatory Compliance Loop

Step 01 • Jan 1st

Fiscal Cycle Commences

Daily logbooks open to record Zakat (alms), Sadaqah (donations), and physical donation box receipts (Tabung).

Step 02 • On/Before April 1st

Hard Statutory Deadline

Every Mutawalli must officially file an itemized statement of accounts for the preceding year with the Waqf Board.

Step 03 • Continuous

AML & Beneficial Ownership

Mosques must continuously log beneficial ownership details, identify major independent donors, and keep logs available for inspection.
